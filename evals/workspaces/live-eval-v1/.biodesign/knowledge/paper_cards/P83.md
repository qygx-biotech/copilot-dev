# Research Question

Evaluation of the mechanical stability and shape retention of a sodium alginate and cellulose hydrogel after cold storage.

# Main Findings

- After 7 days of storage at 4°C, the mean compression modulus of the material was 46.0 kPa.
- The shape retention rate of the material after 7 days of storage at 4°C was 95%.
- The mean compression modulus decreased by 4.0 kPa, representing an 8.0% reduction compared to fresh samples.
- The compression modulus mean was 46.0 kPa (SD 2.0 kPa, n=4) after 7 days of storage at 4°C.
- Fresh samples had a mean compression modulus of 50.0 kPa (n=4).

# Methods

- Formulation of sodium alginate 2.0% and cellulose 1.2%
- Curing with 50 mmol/L calcium chloride for 10 minutes
- Storage at 4°C for 7 days
- Mechanical testing in wet state after 24-hour equilibrium at 10% strain

# Limitations

- No freeze-thaw cycles were performed
- No measurements of cell viability or microbial contamination
- Results from 7-day refrigeration cannot be generalized to freezing conditions or long-term storage

# Short Summary

This study evaluated a sodium alginate and cellulose hydrogel, finding that after 7 days of storage at 4°C, it retained 95% of its shape and experienced only an 8% decrease in compression modulus.
