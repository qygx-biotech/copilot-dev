# Research Question

Evaluation of the properties of nonliving cellulose–alginate hydrogel sheets under different calcium chloride concentrations.

# Main Findings

- The 50 mmol/L condition resulted in a mean compressive modulus of 42.0 kPa, shape retention of 96%, and water uptake of 10.0 g water per g dry sheet.
- The 75 mmol/L condition resulted in a mean compressive modulus of 56.0 kPa and shape retention of 88%.
- Higher compressive modulus was associated with poorer shape retention.
- The 50 mmol/L condition had a mean compressive modulus of 42.0 kPa and 96% shape retention.
- The 75 mmol/L condition had a mean compressive modulus of 56.0 kPa and 88% shape retention.

# Methods

- 2.0% w/v alginate and 0.8% w/v cellulose
- calcium chloride bath 50 mmol/L for 10 minutes
- Compression was measured wet, after 24 hours of equilibration, at 10% strain

# Limitations

- Only two calcium concentrations and one cellulose fraction were evaluated
- No cost, biodegradation, cytotoxicity, cell viability, or printer throughput data are available

# Short Summary

This study evaluated the compressive modulus, shape retention, and water uptake of cellulose-alginate hydrogel sheets crosslinked with 50 mmol/L and 75 mmol/L calcium chloride.
