# Main Findings

- The hydrogel exhibited a mean compressive modulus of 51.0 kPa.
- The hydrogel demonstrated 93% shape retention.
- The water uptake of the hydrogel was 12.5 g/g.
- The reported mean compressive modulus of 51.0 kPa is 9.0 kPa higher than the P17 reported mean of 42.0 kPa.
- Water uptake increased from 10.0 to 12.5 g/g relative to P17.

# Methods

- Curing in 50 mmol/L calcium chloride for 10 minutes
- 24 hours equilibration
- Wet compression at 10% strain

# Limitations

- One of six sheets exhibited visible brittle cracking during handling
- The study did not establish a crack-rate confidence interval
- No living-cell or safety testing occurred

# Short Summary

This study characterizes a nonliving hydrogel composed of 2.0% w/v alginate and 1.2% w/v cellulose, reporting a mean compressive modulus of 51.0 kPa and water uptake of 12.5 g/g.
